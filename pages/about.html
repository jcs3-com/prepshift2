§ 05 · Standing Notice

About the Work

Prepshift was a business, then a methodology, and now a book published page by page. This is what it is, what it isn't, and why it exists.

The bar industry has, since roughly 2005, undergone a quiet professionalization. The "craft cocktail" rebrand brought fresh juice, classic specs, and serious attention to ingredient quality. What it did not bring, with rare exceptions, was operational rigor — the costing, scheduling, training, and quality control that any kitchen of comparable ambition treats as table stakes. Prepshift was built to close that gap.

§ 01

What Prepshift was

2019–2024, more or less

A bar-prep outsourcing service operating out of the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area. The model was straightforward: a central production facility, staffed by someone who actually knew what they were doing, produced cocktail syrups, super juices, fresh-pressed citrus, ginger juice, cordials, foamers, and other prepared ingredients to spec. We costed them at the unit level, tracked seasonal variance, sourced ingredients at scale, and delivered them to client bars on a route schedule with proper labeling, dating, and FIFO discipline.

Client bars got better, more consistent product than they could produce in-house. They got it at a lower true cost than their own labor would have produced it for, once you account for the prep cook hours, the equipment depreciation, the wasted yield from inconsistent technique, and the spoilage from overbatching. They got it without having to train, supervise, or replace the person doing the prep. Most importantly, they got their bartenders' time back — bartenders who could now spend their shift building drinks instead of juicing limes.

Why the model worked Centralized production has every economic advantage over distributed production: better equipment utilization, bulk ingredient sourcing, dedicated labor at lower per-unit cost, quality control by a specialist rather than a generalist. The reason every bar isn't doing this in-house at scale is that "the bar" is the wrong unit of analysis. The right unit is the metropolitan area. That insight, applied to a market that hadn't yet figured it out, is the business.
§ 02

What Prepshift is now

2026 onward

This site, and eventually the book that follows it. The operating business is wound down, but the methodology, the data, and the accumulated knowledge from years of doing this work professionally are not. Publishing them openly serves two purposes.

First, the industry needs better-published methodology. The state of bar education today is YouTube tutorials of varying quality, scattered blog posts, a handful of excellent books (Arnold's Liquid Intelligence; Death & Co's volumes; Cocktail Codex; Kos's online resources; Morgenthaler's writing), and a tradition of mentorship that scales poorly and excludes everyone outside major urban markets. The gap between "best-in-class technique" and "median bar in America" is enormous and growing. Closing some of that gap, with rigorous and publicly available documentation, is worth doing.

Second, the business may not be over so much as it is paused. Documenting the methodology in public is also documenting the moat. If the original service ever scales beyond a single operator and a single city — or if someone else wants to build it elsewhere using the same playbook — the publicly available reference is an asset, not a liability.

§ 03

What this site is not

Common misreadings

Not a cocktail recipe site. There are excellent cocktail recipe resources — Difford's Guide, Imbibe, the various brand-backed databases. This site documents the prep that makes cocktail recipes work. Drink recipes appear only as downstream applications of the prep work.

Not a buying guide. Equipment recommendations appear because they're needed to execute the methodology, not as affiliate-driven product reviews. When affiliate links eventually do exist on this site, they will be disclosed at the page level and at the link level. No sponsored placements, no paid reviews, no SEO-driven product roundups.

Not a brand-friendly platform. Spirit and ingredient brands are named only where naming them serves the methodology. There are no brand integrations, no white-label content, no sponsored stories. If a piece of equipment or a brand is bad, this site will say so. If it's good, this site will say so without being paid to.

Not for beginners exclusively. The tone here assumes the reader knows what a jigger is and has built a drink before. There are good resources for absolute beginners (Morgenthaler's The Bar Book is the canonical one). This site is aimed at the working bartender, the bar owner, the consultant, and the serious enthusiast who has outgrown the beginner material.

§ 04

The status of the data

Honest disclosure

Every cost figure, yield percentage, and shelf-life duration on this site is currently a placeholder. The numbers are calibrated to industry rules of thumb, validated against public sources (Kos, Morgenthaler, Arnold, the Death & Co volumes), and stress-tested against my own approximate recollection of running the actual business. They are useful for structure and for relative comparison. They are not the production data from the actual Prepshift operation.

That data exists, is being compiled, and will replace the current placeholders before this site reaches a v1.0 release. When that happens, every page will carry a "v1.0 · Verified" badge in the masthead area, and the figures will reflect audited actuals from the operating business. Until then: useful, approximate, and clearly labeled as such.

If you found a number that seems wrong Reach out. I'd rather correct it now than print it in a book later. Email contact will appear here once the site moves out of build mode; until then, treat everything as a working draft.

Where this site presents an idea that originated with one of the above, the attribution will appear in the relevant page. Where attribution is missing, it is an oversight, not an assertion of originality.

The bar industry runs on shared knowledge. Hoarding methodology has never made anyone's bar better — sharing it has lifted the entire trade. This site is an attempt to contribute.
§ 06

The book

Forthcoming

The eventual physical product is a book. The structure of this site — chaptered, numbered, sectioned, designed to read in print — is intentional. The aesthetic choices on these pages are not arbitrary; they're the same choices we'll make for the print edition. Publishing here is a way of writing the book in the open, getting feedback from working operators, and arriving at print with a manuscript that has already been used by real bars.

Target publication: ambitious is 2027; honest is 2028. Subscribe to the Stories section as the index is built out, or check back as recipes, calculators, and chapters accumulate. The site will reach v1.0 — verified data, complete reference set, equipment list with sourcing partners — before the book goes to print.
